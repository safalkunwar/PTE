## Findings captured

Pearson currently lists nine Speaking & Writing question types, including Personal Introduction, Read Aloud, Repeat Sentence, Describe Image, Retell Lecture, Answer Short Question, Summarize Group Discussion, Respond to a Situation, Summarize Written Text, and Write Essay. Pearson states that Personal Introduction is unscored and is for familiarization. The official page shows 25 seconds to prepare and 30 seconds to record for Personal Introduction.

Pearson currently lists five Reading question types: Fill in the Blanks (Dropdown), Multiple Choice, Multiple Answers, Reorder Paragraph, Fill in the Blanks (Drag and Drop), and Multiple Choice, Single Answer. The Reading section is listed as 23–30 minutes. Reading and Writing: Fill in the Blanks is identified as an integrated item that also assesses writing skills. Pearson's dropdown fill-in-the-blanks page describes passage text up to 300 words and dropdown selection at each gap.

## Additional findings captured

Pearson currently lists eight Listening question types: Summarize Spoken Text, Multiple Choice Multiple Answers, Fill in the Blanks (Type In), Highlight Correct Summary, Multiple Choice Single Answer, Select Missing Word, Highlight Incorrect Words, and Write from Dictation. Pearson states that audio or video clips begin automatically, each clip is heard once, and note-taking is allowed. Listening is listed as 31–39 minutes. Summarize Spoken Text uses a 60–90 second prompt and requires a 50–70 word summary, with 10 minutes to listen and write.

The current official Score Guide PDF was opened for the audit. Its search result and document title confirm it is the source for correct/incorrect, partial-credit, and form/score rules; the implementation audit must keep those rules distinct rather than treating every item as binary.

## Score and Listening rules from current Pearson sources

Pearson's Listening page states that Listening has eight question types, runs for 31–39 minutes, uses automatically playing audio/video clips, plays each clip once, and allows note-taking. Summarize Spoken Text uses a 60–90 second recording, requires 50–70 words, and provides 10 minutes to listen and write.

The current Pearson Test Taker Score Guide states that PTE Academic scores range from 10–90 and that test takers complete 65–75 questions across 22 question types. It distinguishes binary correct/incorrect scoring from partial credit, where correct responses receive maximum points, partly correct responses receive fewer points, and incorrect responses receive none. It states that form/content scores can gate further scoring; for example, a Describe Image response with zero Content receives no further scoring. It also states that integrated items contribute to both relevant communicative skills. Source: https://www.pearsonpte.com/content/dam/ELL/pte/pearsonpte/pdfs/pte-academic-pdfs/PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf
